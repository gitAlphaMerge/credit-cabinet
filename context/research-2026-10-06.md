# What should be the core of our SME credit agent? Deep research for the Alfa-Future 2026 credit track

Alfa-Future Hackathon 2026, track «Умный кредитный кабинет предпринимателя» (online stage 19–26 Oct). Evidence checked up to 6 Oct 2026. Run: Claude Opus 5.5 on the shared prompt, for the diff with ChatGPT Deep Research.

**How to read this report**

- Every claim carries a source ID in square brackets. Each ID resolves to exactly one URL, listed in section 7; the tables repeat those URLs verbatim.
- Source type is marked in section 7: **P** = primary (regulator, statute text, company page or release, company engineering blog, peer-reviewed paper or arXiv preprint, official dataset page); **S** = secondary (press, law-firm memo, aggregator, review site). Treat S-only claims as leads, not pitch numbers.
- *(consumer)* marks retail-credit evidence, used only where it informs the SME and sole-proprietor case.

## 1. Executive summary

1. Approval odds alone are not new in Russia: Sber's «Кредитный потенциал» already shows IP and OOO clients a pre-approved amount and the factors behind it, refreshed automatically every 30 days for clients with a loan or application history [S15][S16]. T-Bank precomputes offers for 2 million entrepreneurs [S19], and Tochka promises to explain refusals [S23].
2. The bank lists our track as «Персонализировать подбор кредита» (personalise loan selection) [S2][S3], and only a working prototype that implements the case's required functions gets scored [S1].
3. Alfa-Intellect (5 Oct 2026, test mode) routes each request to a specialist agent and gives businesses one window over payments, income and expenses, statements, counterparties and product terms. No credit function is announced [S4][S5][S6]. Alfa and Sber already expose banking functions to AI agents through MCP servers [S10][S18].
4. SME evidence favours cash-flow underwriting: deposits, balances, volatility and low-balance events add signal beyond the owner's credit score, most for young firms [S96][S98][S95]. The US SBA replaced its small-loan score gate with a 1.10 debt-service-coverage floor in March 2026 [S39][S40].
5. Recourse is fragile: model updates invalidate it [S86], advice computed without a causal model can be infeasible [S85][S88], and disclosed levers get gamed [S91].
6. Calling applicants "pre-approved" when many are then declined has already drawn an enforcement order [S47][S48], and a company is liable for what its chatbot tells customers [S49].
7. Russian consumer-credit law expressly allows refusal without reasons, and I found no rule requiring reasons for business loans [S59]; the Bank of Russia's 2025 AI Code recommends human review and explaining what the client must do [S54]. Micro-business default rates are close to 10% [S65].
8. No public dataset joins business cash flows to default labels, so the engine must stay data-agnostic until the case data arrives on 19 Oct [S99][S100][S74][S76].
9. **Recommended core:** a cash-flow "credit readiness" engine. It detects the financing need, picks the instrument, sets a conditional limit and returns 1–3 verifiable, causal actions with an expiry date, all exposed as a tool the assistant calls. Recourse is a layer on top; data-for-terms screening is one of the actions, not the pricing model.
10. **The fact that most supports it:** the bank frames the task as personalised loan selection [S2], while approval odds with factors already exist at the market leader [S15].

## 2. Findings by dimension

### 2.1 Global landscape: products that say why and what to change (2023–2026)

The products I found fall into three patterns, and almost none of them offers individual recourse.

**(a) A score plus generic levers.**
- Capital One's CreditWise simulator estimates how actions such as paying down a card balance would move a FICO Score 8 computed from the user's TransUnion report. It is free, runs on a soft inquiry and is live [S25] *(consumer)*.
- Nav (US) pulls business credit files from D&B, Experian and Equifax and shows scores, payment history and "red flags" before matching owners to lenders. The claim that its matches are 3.5 times more likely to be approved is Nav marketing repeated by a review site [S27]. Nav's own guide tells owners to check approval odds through soft prequalification before applying [S26].

**(b) Conditional offers computed from operating data the provider already sees.**
- Square Loans are invitation-only. Eligibility and offer size depend on processing volume, account history and payment frequency [S28]. Square shows an eligibility page with key factors, but its staff state that an offer is not guaranteed even when every displayed factor reads "Good", and that accounts are re-evaluated daily [S29]. This is the closest live analogue of "factors without a promise".
- QuickBooks Capital underwrites from the accounting books, optionally connected bank accounts and the guarantor's personal credit. Loans run from $1.5K to $200K, and eligibility generally requires about $50K of revenue over 12 months [S30].
- In Russia, Sber's «Кредитный потенциал» shows IP and OOO clients a pre-approved amount. It draws on account turnover and its regularity, the company's and the IP's credit history, business age (two years preferred), sector risk and existing debt. It recalculates automatically every 30 days for clients with an application or loan history, can be refreshed on request, and is framed as something that «повышает шансы на одобрение, но не заменяет полной проверки» [S15]. The tool launched in 2023 [S16].
- T-Business advertises personal offers prepared for 2 million entrepreneurs and caps working-capital loans at 150% of average monthly revenue [S19]. VTB offers pre-approved decisions to existing clients [S22].

**(c) Process rails that route applicants.**
- NatWest (UK) runs an eligibility check that returns an indicative quote, and it publishes how accurate those quotes are: 84.16% of SME customers received a final quote equal to, better than, or no more than 10% above the indicative one [S31]. Publishing calibration is a pattern worth copying.
- India's PSB Loans in 59 Minutes issues in-principle approval from GST returns, income-tax returns and bank statements, with fraud and bureau checks; the bank keeps the final sanction [S32][S33] (both S).
- Since November 2016 the UK Bank Referral Scheme has obliged designated banks to offer declined SMEs a referral to designated finance platforms [S34]. Three platforms are designated, and the government opened a consultation in October 2025 [S35]. The 2024 post-implementation review notes that many referred firms are rejected again [S36]. UK Finance puts take-up at 2–3% of declined applicants and success at about 6% of those referred (reported second-hand) [S37].

**Policy gates moved from scores to cash-flow coverage.** The SBA raised the minimum FICO SBSS score for streamlined 7(a) Small Loans from 155 to 165 in June 2025 [S38]. On 1 March 2026 it ended mandatory SBSS prescreening and replaced it with underwriting that includes a minimum 1.10 debt-service-coverage ratio [S39][S40] (both S).

**Why small firms are refused.** In the Fed's survey covering 2024, 41% of firms denied financing said existing debt was the reason, up from 22% in 2021 [S42]. In the survey covering 2025, 22% of applicants received nothing and 42% received the full amount [S41].

**Documented failures.** Kabbage automated PPP lending, dropped underwriting steps and relied on weak automated fraud checks; it went bankrupt in October 2022 and settled for up to $120M in May 2024 [S43]. Credit Karma's "pre-approved" claims are covered in section 2.5 [S47].

**Implication for our product:** the market converged on conditional offers computed from verified operating data, with coarse factors and explicit disclaimers. Nobody I found promises individual "do X and you are approved" recourse, most likely because of liability and invalidation risk. Our edge has to be honest, verifiable actions attached to a conditional offer, not the approval number itself, which Sber already shows.

### 2.2 Bank AI assistants with agent architectures

**Alfa.**
- Alfa-Intellect launched on 5 October 2026 in test mode for thousands of app users. One assistant takes a single message and connects the right specialist agent. For business it works as a single window over current and upcoming payments, income and expenses, statements, the history with a given counterparty and the terms of bank products [S4][S5][S6]. Separate agents act as lawyer, accountant or marketer [S4]. The next steps named are agent payments within a client-set limit, shopping and food-delivery agents, and "more business tasks" [S7].
- None of the four reports mentions credit eligibility, a loan application or an approval chance [S4][S5][S6][S7]. The plan for cash-flow forecasting and cash-gap risk in our brief does not appear in these sources: **OPEN**.
- Infrastructure already in place:
  - MCP servers that let AI agents query banking services in natural language, launched for legal entities in May 2026. The published example is FX rates; no lending functions are listed [S10][S11].
  - A currency-control agent built on Alfa's AlfaGen and AIDA platforms, with a stated plan for a multi-agent system by the end of 2026 [S12].
  - An AI platform for corporate credit analysis that covers 65% of new corporate deals and decides from 45 minutes, while a human takes the final decision [S8][S9].

**Other Russian banks.**
- Sber: the SberBusiness Giga-assistant runs more than 20 agents and pushes hints built from the company's financial data, revenue dynamics and market trends, including growth-planning and tender agents [S17]. Since June 2026 a SberBusiness MCP lets AI agents create rouble payments and pull statements [S18]. Credit potential is offered as a separate service inside SberBusiness [S15]; I found no sign that the assistant answers eligibility questions.
- T-Bank: the consumer assistant «Тая» (beta) recommends products even from other banks and is meant to take income, spending and debt burden into account later [S20][S21]. Business credit offers are precomputed [S19].
- Tochka: the business assistant executes transfers and opens deposits from chat [S24]. Its own SME loans come with a promise to explain refusals [S23].
- VTB: pre-approved SME decisions exist [S22]; I found no assistant-level credit function (**OPEN**).
- The regulator's view: the Bank of Russia's November 2025 consultation paper reports that AI agents are still used cautiously, mostly in IT and chatbots [S55].

**Global.**
- Capital One's Chat Concierge splits work between agents. One talks to the customer, one plans from business rules, an evaluator simulates the plan against policies before anything executes, and one explains the result. It runs on a customised Llama model [S44].
- Bank of America added generative AI to EricaAssist, used by more than 18,000 employees, under human oversight (July 2026) [S45].
- NatWest is rolling out an agentic assistant in Cora, built on OpenAI models, for spending questions and fraud cases (S) [S46].

**Finding.** None of the assistants reviewed handles credit eligibility or "how to get approved". In Russia, pre-approval lives in product screens (Sber, T-Bank, VTB), not in assistants. Whether any Russian assistant answers eligibility questions is **OPEN**.

**Implication for our product:** the gap the brief assumes is real but narrow: the assistant and the pre-approval screen both exist, and nobody has joined them. Build the credit agent as a deterministic tool with a typed contract that an orchestrator or an MCP client can call. Keep Capital One's plan, evaluate, explain separation, and give every answer a path to a human.

### 2.3 ML for SME credit decisions

**SME-specific evidence.**
- FinRegLab's 2025 study covered more than 38,000 small-business loans from two fintech lenders (February 2015 to January 2024). Cash-flow data substantially increased predictive signal over models built on the owner's personal credit score and firm characteristics, most for young firms with low-score owners [S96]. Deposits and balances were the strongest variables; withdrawals, balance volatility and low or negative balance events were also predictive [S98]. Its 2019 study pointed the same way on earlier data [S97].
- On 1.8 million MYbank loans, an IMF working paper found that big data plus machine learning (random forest) predicted defaults better than a bank-style scorecard on standard financials, including during shocks, and helped the smallest firms most [S95].
- US policy moved the same way when the SBA swapped its score gate for a debt-service-coverage floor in 2026 [S39].

**Russian practice.**
- In the Bank of Russia's November 2025 survey, 62% of respondents use or test AI for credit scoring, as do 11 of 12 systemically important banks, and «уровень "самостоятельности" ИИ в скоринге физических лиц и МСП фактически приближается к 100%» [S55].
- At the July 2026 Financial Congress, a Bank of Russia official said logistic regression remains the standard for prudential models because it is interpretable (reported speech) [S56].
- Alfa's own engineering blog (2021, retail): gradient boosting on hand-built transaction aggregates reached a ROC AUC of 0.752. A sequence neural network did slightly worse on its own but added signal, and it runs in production as an additive component [S13] *(consumer)*.

**Features that carry signal** (synthesis of [S96][S98][S15]): the level and stability of turnover; balance dynamics and low-balance days; debt service against inflows; tax-payment discipline and arrears; counterparty concentration; business age; and, for an IP, the owner's credit history, which Sber scores explicitly [S15].

**Performance ranges.** I found no published AUC for a Russian SME probability-of-default model. The only bank-published figure is Alfa's retail 0.752 [S13]. **OPEN.**

**Public datasets, closest to real SME underwriting first.**
- Sber's MBD: about 2 million corporate clients with transactions, geodata and dialogue embeddings. The target is product purchase, there are no default labels, and the licence is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 [S76].
- RFSD: annual financial statements of all Russian firms for 2011–2023, 56.6 million firm-years, with non-filers flagged [S74]; the repository extends coverage to 2025 [S75]. It has no default labels, and IPs are absent because they do not file statements.
- SBA 7(a)/504 FOIA: loan-level outcomes including charge-offs, refreshed to 30 September 2025, but almost no borrower financials [S99].
- UCI Polish bankruptcy: financial ratios of companies rather than micro-firms [S100].
- Give Me Some Credit: consumer data only [S101].
- Alfa's credit-history competition: a ROC AUC task; whether the data stayed available afterwards is **OPEN** [S14].

**Implication for our product:** use an interpretable scorecard (logistic regression on binned cash-flow and bureau features) as the primary model, with a monotone gradient-boosting challenger [S102] kept only if it clearly wins. That gives exact reason codes and exact recourse, and it matches what Russian risk executives call the standard [S56]. Demo on a public proxy, but design every feature for bank transactions so the case data drops straight in.

### 2.4 Explainability and actionable recourse

**State of the art.**
- Counterfactual explanations describe the smallest change to the inputs that would flip a decision [S84]. Ustun et al. turned this into actionable recourse for linear classifiers: integer programming over the features a person can actually change, priced by cost, plus an audit of who has no recourse at all [S83].
- Changing a feature in the model is not the same as acting in the world. Without a causal model, recommended actions can be suboptimal or impossible to carry out [S85]. Counterfactuals also rest on hidden assumptions: that features move independently, that each action maps to one feature, and that the model stays fixed [S88].
- Recourse should improve the real outcome (the ability to repay), not just the prediction; otherwise it coaches people to game the model [S89]. A classifier shapes the effort people invest, and it can be designed with that in mind [S90].

**Known pitfalls.**
- *Instability.* Retraining after temporal, geographic or data-correction shifts can invalidate recourse already given, and cheap recourse trades off against recourse that survives updates [S86]. Robust methods accept a little more cost in exchange for validity [S87].
- *Manipulable explanations.* Post-hoc attributions such as SHAP and LIME can be gamed to hide what a model relies on [S93], and Shapley-value importance has conceptual problems as an explanation [S94].
- *Gameability.* See the field evidence in section 2.5 [S91].

**Ranking recourse by real-world cost.** Ustun et al. price actions by percentile shifts in the population [S83]. For SMEs I would rank by three things, in order: whether the bank can verify the action, how long it takes to show up in the data, and how much money it costs. *Judgment.*

**Design rules that follow.**
1. Separate *reasons* (everything pulling the score down, including what cannot change) from *actions* (only levers that are actionable, causal and verifiable).
2. Compute actions against a versioned model and give them an expiry date; Sber recalculates every 30 days [S15]. Re-check at application.
3. Aim above the threshold with a margin, so that a retrain does not void the advice [S86][S87].
4. Never show SHAP values as customer-facing reasons; use scorecard points or monotone-model deltas [S93][S94].
5. If no cheap action works within a set number of months, route to another instrument.

**Implication for our product:** a recourse simulator is defensible only as a narrow, versioned, verifiable layer on a scorecard or monotone model. As the core of the product it carries the most risk for the least novelty.

### 2.5 Mechanism-design view

**Menus of contracts.** Classic screening theory shows that a menu (rate against collateral) lets borrowers sort themselves (Bester 1985, *AER* 75(4); URL not verified in this run). In our setting the practical menu is verification tiers: own-bank turnover, then statements from other banks, then FNS tax data with consent, then a bureau report, each unlocking a firmer or larger offer. *Judgment.*

**Refusing to share is itself a signal, and that has a cost.** He, Huang and Zhou show that once borrowers can share their bank data, the choice to share reveals their type, and open banking can leave the industry better off while leaving all borrowers worse off [S92]. If we price non-sharers down, everyone with good data shares and everyone else is pooled as risky. That raises fairness and reputational questions, and for an IP every shared item is personal data.

**Gaming is real and measurable.** In a Kenyan field experiment with 1,557 smartphone users over 16 weeks, disclosing the decision rule raised the standard model's prediction error by about 23%, against about 8% for a manipulation-robust estimator [S91]. For our levers this means:
- "Route turnover through Alfa" is the easiest lever to game, through circular transfers. Measure quality (distinct counterparties, regularity, taxes paid) rather than volume. *Judgment.*
- Debt repayment is verifiable in the bureau, and closed tax arrears in FNS open data (section 2.7).
- Self-reported revenue or purpose is cheap talk: use it for routing, never for scoring. *Judgment.*

**The bank's risk when advice turns out wrong.**
- Credit Karma used "pre-approved" wording after A/B tests showed it drew more clicks than "excellent odds", although for some offers nearly a third of applicants were then denied. The FTC ordered $3M in redress and banned false pre-approval and approval-odds claims [S47][S48].
- A Canadian tribunal held Air Canada liable for wrong information its website chatbot gave a customer [S49].
- The CFPB warns that a poorly deployed chatbot can mislead customers, trap them in loops and break the law [S50].
- NYDFS found no fair-lending violation in the Apple Card case, but said service deficiencies and a perceived lack of transparency undermined trust in the credit decisions [S51]. Separately, in October 2024 the CFPB penalised Apple and Goldman Sachs because disputes customers raised through the card's interface never reached the bank [S52]: the interface layer alone can create liability.

**Design principles stated publicly.** Capital One evaluates every plan against policies before acting [S44]. Bank of America keeps humans overseeing AI guidance [S45]. Alfa keeps a human on the final corporate credit decision [S9]. NatWest publishes how accurate its indicative quotes are [S31]. The Bank of Russia Code recommends review by an employee and explaining what the client must do [S54].

**Implication for our product:** use the menu idea for verification tiers ("sharing X would let us offer up to Y"), but never penalise a refusal beyond "we cannot assess X". Never show "approved": every statement is conditional, versioned and backed by a published calibration figure, and the record of any A/B tests is kept.

### 2.6 Regulation, Russia first

**Explaining refusals.**
- I found no statute obliging a bank to explain why it refused a business loan (*Judgment*, within the sources reviewed). For consumer credit the law is explicit: «Кредитор может отказать заемщику в заключении договора потребительского кредита (займа) без объяснения причин» (353-FZ, art. 7 part 5). The same article limits that law to borrowing for non-business purposes, so an IP's business loan falls outside it [S59].
- The Bank of Russia's Code of Ethics for AI (9 July 2025) is recommendatory and rests on five principles: human-centricity, fairness, transparency, security/reliability/efficiency and responsible risk management. It recommends telling clients when AI is used (4.2), letting them deal with an employee (2.3) and having an employee review AI decisions (2.4). Closest to recourse, it recommends to «разъяснять действия, которые клиенту необходимо совершить для принятия искусственным интеллектом решения» (2.5) [S54]. The Bank of Russia says more than 80% of organisations that use AI already let clients switch to a human [S53].
- The November 2025 consultation paper keeps a risk-based, technology-neutral approach and finds near-full automation of retail and SME scoring (quoted in 2.3) [S55]. In July 2026 the regulator described its line as soft regulation: monitor, then recommend, then require only if needed (reported) [S56]. Methodological recommendations 3-MR on information security in AI (16 June 2026) exist, but I did not review their content: **OPEN** [S57].

**Personal data and bank secrecy.**
- Since 1 September 2025, consent to process personal data must be «оформлено отдельно от иных информации и (или) документов» (Law 156-FZ amending 152-FZ) [S58]. An IP is a natural person, so IP data is personal data (*Judgment*; interpretation not checked against a source).
- Bank secrecy (art. 26 of the banking law) and whether client data may be sent to an external LLM API were not reviewed in this run: **OPEN**. Design assumption: models and any LLM run inside the bank's perimeter.

**Credit histories, IP versus LLC.**
- 218-FZ art. 4 sets out separate title-part contents for individuals, for individual entrepreneurs (adding the IP registration number) and for legal entities [S60].
- A bank needs the subject's consent to obtain a credit report; the consent is valid for six months, and individuals get two free reports a year [S61].
- Whether an IP's business loans sit in the same bureau file as personal loans is **OPEN**. In practice, Sber's tool scores the IP's credit history [S15].
- The credit self-ban, in force since 1 March 2025, covers consumer loans including overdrafts and credit cards; the Bank of Russia does not describe it as covering business loans [S62].

**Open banking.** Mandatory data exchange through Open API is postponed beyond 2026, with dates to follow a federal law still being drafted [S63]. Within our timeframe, data from other banks arrives only through statements the client uploads.

**SME lending context.**
- Overdue debt was 3.9% of SME debt on 1 August 2026, down from 4.2% a month earlier [S64].
- According to NCR, one SME borrower in six was in arrears on 1 May 2026 and overdue debt stood at 4.1%. Over 12 months the default rate rose from 8.1% to 9.9% for micro-firms and from 3.7% to 5.9% for small firms, and new issuance fell 15% in 2025 [S65].
- The Bank of Russia put problem loans (categories IV–V) at 7.6% of the SME portfolio (reported by a press-release site) [S66].
- On one marketplace, Sravni, 7% of SMB applications were approved in H1 2026. That is a marketplace funnel, not the whole market [S67].
- A regulator-published SME approval or refusal rate: not found, **OPEN**.

**Foreign contrast.**
- *EU.* AI systems that assess the creditworthiness of natural persons are high-risk under Annex III, point 5(b) [S79]. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published on 24 July 2026, moved those obligations to 2 December 2027 [S78], following a political agreement in May 2026 [S77]. "Natural persons" covers sole proprietors but not LLCs, so the IP boundary matters in the EU too.
- *CJEU.* In C-203/22 (27 February 2025) the court held that a person scored automatically is entitled to a description of the "procedure and principles actually applied", and that handing over the algorithm is not enough [S80].
- *US.* Regulation B requires that a statement of reasons "must be specific and indicate the principal reason(s)", and for business applicants with revenues up to $1M the notice may be oral or written [S82]. The CFPB withdrew its 2022 and 2023 circulars on reasons for algorithmic decisions on 12 May 2025, but the rule itself stands [S81].

**Implication for our product:** in Russia, explaining a refusal is a product choice with a soft regulatory tailwind (Code 2.5), so pitch it as a trust feature rather than as compliance. Design to the strictest analogue anyway: specific reasons, a human-review path, separate consent screens, and data that never leaves the perimeter.

### 2.7 Russian market fit

**What a Russian bank can see** (detail in section 5):
- its own client's turnover, payments and counterparties, which Alfa-Intellect already aggregates [S5];
- other banks' statements, only through client upload while Open API is postponed [S63];
- the bureau report, with a six-month consent [S61];
- FNS open data (headcount, taxes paid, arrears, tax regime, statements), plus tax-secret data the taxpayer releases on consent form KND 1110058 [S68][S69];
- harmonised firm statements in RFSD, for LLCs only [S74];
- the self-employed income certificate from «Мой налог»: **OPEN**.

**Where the agent can route when a loan is the wrong answer.**
- Umbrella guarantees from the SME Corporation, for firms that lack collateral: about 1.4 trillion roubles raised by more than 105,000 entrepreneurs since September 2021 [S70], with fresh limits in 2026 [S71].
- Subsidised programmes: 6.5% of loans issued to SMEs as of 1 April 2026 [S65].
- Leasing and bank guarantees: 8.6% and 6.3% of SMB applications on the Sravni marketplace in H1 2026 [S67].
- Inside Alfa's ecosystem: the Potok crowdlending platform (latest source dated 2023; 2026 status **OPEN**) [S72] and an Alfa–MSP Bank agreement on digital bonds for SMEs (details **OPEN**) [S73]. Factoring and overdraft terms for Alfa's SME segment were not sourced: **OPEN**.

**What does not transfer, and why.**
- Mandatory referral after a decline (UK): Russia has no such mandate, and even in the UK most referred firms are declined again [S36][S37].
- Open-banking credit builders: the Russian Open API is postponed [S63].
- Adverse-action notices (US): there is no Russian equivalent, and consumer law even allows silent refusal [S59].
- A national SME score such as FICO SBSS: none exists in Russia, and the SBA itself dropped the mandate [S39].
- GST-style universal invoice data (India's PSB59): Russia has the FNS consent route but no open real-time API, so this transfers only in part [S32][S68].

**What transfers:** pre-approval from operating data, already standard at Sber and T-Bank [S15][S19]; debt-service-coverage capacity checks [S39]; and published calibration [S31].

**Implication for our product:** build on the data Alfa already holds (turnover, counterparties, payments) plus two consented sources, the bureau and the FNS. Route by blocker: a collateral gap goes to a loan with an umbrella guarantee, a seasonal gap to an overdraft or credit line, equipment to leasing.

### 2.8 Synthesis: three candidate cores

| | **A. Recourse simulator** | **B. Contract-menu screening** | **C. Cash-flow credit readiness (recommended)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the client gets | Approval chance, the 3 factors pulling it down, the cheapest path to approval | Offer tiers: "share X → limit or price Y" | "You need about X by date D for Z. We can offer instrument I, up to L now (conditional) and up to L′ after actions 1–3. Valid until T." |
| Evidence for | Recourse literature [S83]; Code 2.5 [S54]; Tochka's promise [S23] | Screening theory; signalling [S92] | Cash-flow signal [S96][S98][S95]; coverage gate [S39]; incumbents' pre-approvals [S15][S19]; track framing [S2] |
| Build in 8 days at 512 MB | Scorecard plus a grid search over ≤4 levers; feasible | Needs a PD and a price per tier; public data cannot calibrate the tiers | Feature pipeline, scorecard, rule-based need detection and instrument map, limit from coverage, recourse layer; feasible |
| What an executive will ask | "Will we honour the advice? What happens after a retrain? Aren't we teaching people to game us? Who pays if they follow it and are still refused?" | "How do you price the tiers? Isn't penalising non-sharers a reputational risk? What is the conversion uplift?" | "How is this different from Sber's «Кредитный потенциал»? How calibrated is 'likely'? How does it plug into Alfa-Intellect? What if the case has no transactions?" |
| Main risk | Liability and invalidation [S47][S86]; gaming [S91] | Unravelling and fairness [S92]; no data to calibrate | Novelty against incumbents [S15]; dependence on transaction data in the case |

**Recommendation.** Build C as the spine. Add a narrow version of A as its "explain and improve" layer, limited to verifiable, causal actions that are versioned and dated. Fold B in as one of those actions ("connect your other bank's statement or your FNS data to unlock up to Y"). The pitch line: *Sber shows you a number; we tell you what you need, what fits, what we can do now, and the one verifiable step that changes it.*

**How it plugs into the assistant.**
1. The engine is a deterministic service (scorecard, policy rules, recourse search) with no LLM in the decision path.
2. It exposes four tools to the orchestrator, or as an MCP server, the pattern Alfa and Sber already use [S10][S18]: `credit_readiness`, `what_if`, `offer_menu`, `explain`.
3. Each call returns a structured payload: a decision ID, the model version, a status from a closed list (never "approved"), the instrument, the limits now and after conditions, an expiry date, reasons, actions, alternatives and a human-review flag.
4. The LLM only puts the payload into words. Every number it says must exist in the payload, and a template fallback covers the case where the LLM fails or an API key expires.
5. A "talk to a person / request a review" action is always offered (Code 2.3–2.4 [S54]).

An example payload (illustrative values):

```json
{
  "decision_id": "cr-000123",
  "model_version": "scorecard-v3+policy-v2",
  "status": "conditional_preapproval",
```

**Build sketch for C within the constraints** (*Judgment*; the memory figures are my estimates, not sourced):
- A Python model service (scikit-learn scorecard, optional LightGBM monotone challenger) at roughly 150–250 MB, a Go API at about 20 MB and a static front end fit within 512 MB as long as no LLM runs locally.
- Recourse: a grid over at most 4 levers × 5 levels is 625 rescorings, which takes milliseconds.
- Need detection: project monthly inflows and outflows with a seasonal-naive model and flag a gap where the projected balance turns negative.
- Limit: a coverage-style cap (projected inflows cover debt service at least 1.1 times, by analogy with [S39]), bounded by a share of average monthly revenue; T-Business's 150% is a market reference point [S19].
- Calibration: show bands ("likely / possible / unlikely") with the backtested approval rate for each band, NatWest-style [S31].
- If the case data has no transactions: generate transactions from the case's aggregates, label them as synthetic in the demo, and keep the feature contract unchanged.

**What to cut from the current design:** SHAP as customer-facing reasons [S93][S94]; raw "turnover through Alfa" as a lever [S91]; a precise "approval chance %" without calibration [S47]; any LLM in the decision path.

**Implication for our product:** the decision is C, with A and B as layers. That fits the case's likely required function (personalised selection), sets us apart from Sber, and keeps the bank's liability bounded.

## 6. OPEN questions

**For the organisers**

1. Will the case data include account transactions (and for how many months), bureau attributes and a default label? Which default definition (for example, 90+ days past due within 12 months), and is it split by IP and OOO?
2. Which "required functions" (rule 8.2.3) will the case list: approval probability, reasons, recommendations, product selection, an application flow, integration with an assistant?
3. Does rule 13.1 (third-party intellectual property only as illustration or quotation) allow open-source libraries and public datasets (SBA FOIA, UCI Polish, RFSD, Kaggle) inside the solution? Is a CC BY-NC-ND dataset such as Sber's MBD compatible with transferring exclusive rights to Alfa (rule 12.4)?
4. Will judges run the container offline? Are external LLM APIs allowed at judging time, and whose keys should they use? In 2025 several teams' keys expired.
5. Is there a mock of Alfa-Intellect's agent interface, or of Alfa's MCP servers, that a team could integrate with?
6. Will judges test "what-if" advice, for example by re-scoring a profile after the recommended actions?
7. Which metrics will count: ROC AUC or Gini, calibration, stability (PSI), latency, quality of explanations?

**For the bank**

8. Does Alfa plan cash-flow forecasting or cash-gap risk inside Alfa-Intellect, as our brief assumes? The public announcements do not say so.
9. What is Alfa's current policy on explaining SME loan refusals, and on "pre-approved" wording in the app?
10. Which instruments may an agent route to within Alfa (overdraft, credit line, leasing, factoring, loans with umbrella guarantees, Potok, digital bonds with MSP Bank), and under what eligibility rules?
11. How does Alfa treat an IP's personal loans, and a small OOO owner's personal credit history, in SME scoring? Are an IP's business and personal histories one bureau file?
12. Does Alfa already use FNS tax-secret data (KND 1110058) or client-uploaded statements from other banks in SME scoring?
13. Is Alfa piloting cross-bank account data exchange (Open API) that a credit agent could use in 2027?
14. Which LLMs may process client data (Alfa's own model, GigaChat, YandexGPT), given 152-FZ and bank secrecy?
15. What approval and default rates does Alfa see in micro-business, compared with the market's micro default rate of about 10% [S65]?
16. Is Potok still part of Alfa's SME offer in 2026, and what is the status of the digital-bond agreement with MSP Bank?
